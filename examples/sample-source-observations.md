# Sample Source Observations

These observations summarize how the supplied sample kneeboards affected v2 of the design system.

## WWII samples

The Spitfire and Fw 190 examples show that WWII kneeboards need:

- a strong normal checklist format
- a compact weapons page
- support for engine management values
- support for short notes and condition rows
- critical action highlighting
- optional/muted procedural items

## Cold War jet samples

F-5E, MiG-21bis, Mirage F1, and similar checklist styles suggest that Cold War aircraft require:

- normal checklist pages
- combat/fence pages
- navigation/reference pages
- weapons employment pages
- occasional sensor/radar pages
- conversion/reference tables

Cold War kneeboards should feel more technical and less parchment-like than WWII.

## F-14 samples

F-14 material adds the need for:

- carrier operations as a first-class format
- two-seat/sensor page support
- radar/weapon distinction
- launch/trap quick cards

## F-16 sample

F-16 material confirms that modern aircraft should not cram everything into a normal checklist. It needs:

- normal checklist
- combat fence
- sensors/avionics
- weapons employment
- navigation/mission data

## AH-64D sample

The AH-64D suite strongly influenced the v2 revision.

It shows that modern aircraft, especially helicopters, need reference-heavy support:

- day/night visual variants
- station-specific pages
- symbol legends
- cockpit/control maps
- weapon codes
- radio/reference tables
- mission data cards

This is why v2 adds `cockpit-controls`, `sensors-and-avionics`, `reference-tables`, and `mission-data-card` as major format families.
